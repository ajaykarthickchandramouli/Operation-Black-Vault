In Sri Vaishnava philosophy, Sriman Narayana is understood as possessing infinite auspicious qualities (ananta-kalyāṇa-guṇas) and being entirely free from all defects (heyapratyanīka). His perfection is not merely the absence of faults; it also encompasses the fullness of every auspicious attribute.
The digit zero is the key to this clue. In the investigation, it represents the absence of defects—not an absence of qualities, greatness, or existence. The distinction is essential: the number is a device used by the puzzle, while the theological principle concerns the Lord's complete freedom from imperfections and possession of infinite auspicious qualities.
Thus, the first digit is 0.

The clues point to Sri Ranganathaswamy Temple, the celebrated abode of Sri Ranganatha at Srirangam, an island formed by the Kaveri and Kollidam rivers. Here, the Lord reclines upon Ādiśeṣa with His head oriented towards the south—a distinctive feature of the temple's sacred tradition.
Srirangam holds a pre-eminent place in the Sri Vaishnava tradition and is revered as the foremost among the 108 Divya Desams. E-03 — The Unspoken Foundation

In the Sri Vaishnava tradition, prapatti (surrender to Sriman Narayana) is a path of seeking refuge in the Lord, recognising Him as the ultimate protector. The tradition describes five aṅgas, or supporting limbs, associated with this act of surrender:
1. Ānukūlyasya saṅkalpaḥ — Resolving to do what is pleasing to the Lord.
2. Prātikūlyasya varjanam — Refraining from what is displeasing to Him.
3. Kārpaṇyam — Recognising one's helplessness and dependence upon Him.
4. Mahāviśvāsaḥ — Having firm confidence that the Lord will protect the seeker.
5. Goptṛtva-varaṇam — Choosing the Lord as one's protector.
These five limbs support prapatti, while ātma-nikṣepa, the entrusting of oneself entirely to the Lord, is often described as its central act. This distinction is important: the five aṅgas are not five separate paths, but the supporting elements associated with surrender.
The number five represents these five aṅgas. The clue directs the investigator towards the doctrine of prapatti and the understanding that true surrender rests upon recognising the Lord as the refuge and protector. Thus, the third digit is 5.

E-04 — The Marks of the Tradition

Correct Answer: 3
The final clue brings together two important concepts preserved within the Sri Vaishnava tradition: Tattva-trayam, the three fundamental realities, and the Tridandam, the threefold staff associated with Sri Vaishnava ascetic renunciation.
Tattva-trayam explains reality through three fundamental categories:
Chit — Sentient beings, possessing consciousness.
Achit — Insentient matter, which lacks consciousness.
Ishvara — The Supreme Lord, Sriman Narayana, the inner ruler and ultimate support of both sentient beings and insentient matter.

Together, these three realities provide a foundational framework for understanding the relationship between the individual self, the material world, and the Supreme Lord.
The Tridandam, meanwhile, is a staff consisting of three symbolic parts and is an insignia associated with Sri Vaishnava sannyasis. It represents a distinct aspect of the tradition: the discipline and commitment of renunciation.
Although Tattva-trayam and Tridandam belong to different contexts—one to philosophical understanding and the other to the insignia of renunciation—both are connected by the number three.
The clue therefore asks the investigator to recognise their shared numerical significance rather than confuse their meanings.
Thus, the fourth and final digit is 3, completing the PIN: 0153.
